# Atoms - Full

Study-style atoms: the concept absorbed more or less fully, quotes collected at the end. Pick this or `Atoms - Short`.

1. **The lead contains the definition** — never a "Definition" subsection. In the scientific voice it carries the defining equation, numbered (1).
2. **Absorb what the sources say** about the concept: properties, variants, how it's computed or applied, how well it works, where it breaks. No long derivations; name the steps of a short one.
3. **`##` subsections whose titles say what the section shows** (*Uniqueness Holds Under Kruskal's Condition*, *ALS Can Stall in Swamps*), not generic labels (*Properties*, *Limits*). The source's own headings are good hints.
4. **Cite the authority in the prose,** not the source note: the book or paper a statement comes from. Paraphrase; don't put a source note's wording in an author's mouth.
5. **Then** `## Related`, `## References` (if any), and `## Provenance`: per subsection, one to three short quotes from the source notes. Below them, under "Errata in the sources, corrected here:", every error found in a source note and how the atom corrects it.

```markdown
# CP Decomposition

<Why it matters.> <Definition, with its defining equation in the scientific voice.>

## Uniqueness Holds Under Kruskal's Condition

…

## Related

- [[Kruskal Rank]]

## References

1. …

## Provenance

- *Uniqueness Holds Under Kruskal's Condition:* "…" ([[Kolda and Bader 2009#Uniqueness]])
```
